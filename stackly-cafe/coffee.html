The Basics

Coffee Is A Fruit Juice, Honestly

Everything we call coffee starts as the seed inside the cherry of a shrub. On the hillsides of Chikmagalur, Coorg and the Nilgiris, families hand-pick only the ripe red cherries. Those cherries are pulped, fermented in tank for up to 36 hours, then dried on raised beds until the parchment reaches 11% moisture.

What is left is the green bean — a seed with no aroma at all. Roasting is what unlocks roughly 800 aromatic compounds that were never present in the raw seed. Green beans smell like hay. Roasted beans smell like the reason you walked into a cafe.

  • Species — around 130 exist, but Arabica covers 70% of the world crop and almost all of ours.
  • Altitude — slower growth above 1,200 m packs more sugar into the seed.
  • Acidity — the brightness. It is a flavour, not a defect, and it fades fast once roasted.
  • Body — the weight in your mouth. Higher altitude usually means a lighter, silkier body.

The Process

Cherry To Cup, In Four Moves

The same four steps we run every week at the Roastery Lane drum roaster.

1. Selective Harvest

Ripe cherries only, picked by hand across two passes. Machine harvest strips fruit and soil together, and soil tastes like soil.

2. Process & Dry

Washed, honey or natural. Dried on raised beds and turned by hand every two hours until 11% moisture.

3. Roast

Small batches on a 12 kg drum. First crack at 9:40, dropped at 11:15, rested three days before brewing.

4. Brew

Ground to order. Filter brews at 93 °C in three minutes; espresso at 36 seconds. Miss either and it tastes flat.
